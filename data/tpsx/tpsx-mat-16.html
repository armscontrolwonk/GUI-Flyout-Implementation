CFBI Blanket

Name: CFBI Blanket
Database: NASA Ames Thermal Protection Materials
Category: Silicon-Based Reusable Composites: Composite Flexible Blankets
Composition: Nextel 440 (or Nicalon NLM 202) outer fabric, Saffil alumina insulation, and S-glass inner fabric, sewn together with a Nextel 440 (or Nicalon) thread.
Manufacturer: HiTemp Insulations, Oceaneering Thermal Systems
Technical Readiness Level: 7
Last Modified: 2001-10-01

Description:

  • A development of the basic AFRSI TPS architecture, CFBI is a quilted blanket TPS consisting of a woven alumina-borosilicate (or Nicalon NLM 202) outer fabric, a woven glass inner fabric, and fibrous alumina batting sewn together with alumina-borosilicate (or Nicalon) thread. A grouping of aluminized polyimide film reflective layers with alternating aluminoborosilicate scrim fabric, collectively called multi-layer insulation (MLI), is added to the back (inner) surface of the blanket and attached with quilting stitches. For most applications, a water-proofing agent would typically be applied to CFBI at the factory and again between flights. Also, a surface coating of a ceramic material such as C-9 would most likely be required. In addition to meeting emittance requirements, the coating makes the outer surface more rigid and helps to protect the blanket's top surface fibers from aerodynamically-induced abrasion. Compared to tile-type TPS, CFBI's installed cost is low, but its aerodynamic capabilities are far more limited. It has a much rougher surface finish than most tiles and therefore has an increased propensity to force a laminar boundary layer into turbulence. This increased roughness can also produce markedly amplified local heating where boundary layers are relatively thin, such as the windward side of most reentry vehicles. Additionally, CFBI has lower tensile and shear strength compared to tiles, making it less applicable in areas of high aerodynamic loading; especially rapidly fluctuating aerodynamic loads. Although the blanket is very flexible during installation, its surface becomes hardened after application of the C-9 coating and brittle after thermal exposure. Data presented here are for a system consisting of an outer layer of Nicalon fabric, followed by alumina mat insulation, and alternating layers of aluminized polyimide film and aluminoborosilicate scrim fabric.
